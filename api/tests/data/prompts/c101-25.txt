Next, you have the description of the problem:
We have 25 customers, located with their (X,Y) coordinates, all measured in distance units from the origin. The vehicles start from the depot (Customer 0) in the position (40,50), and need to attend to the customers. We can use at most 25 vehicles, all leaving the depot at time 0; they must be back at the depot no later than 1236; arriving later counts as LATE ARRIVAL TIME at the depot. Each vehicle has a capacity of 200, and each customer has a DEMAND that is loaded in the vehicle that serves it. The sum of the demands of the customers of one route must not exceed the capacity. The vehicles altogether must reach all customers.
We are looking for the optimal routes in terms of the score that the vehicles must take in order to deliver to all customers, if possible. The calculation of the score is explained later.
To simplify the problem, we will consider a uniform vehicle velocity of 1 distance unit per time unit, so the travel time between two points equals the travel distance. Key concepts: The time window of the customer is explicit in the READY TIME and DUE TIME columns, meaning that the customer is only available for delivery between the READY TIME value and the DUE TIME value. These values are expressed in time units. The SERVICE TIME column is the other critical one, it represents the time that the vehicles need to deliver to the customer and it is also expressed in time units. In other words, the delivery must start between READY TIME and DUE TIME and the delivery lasts SERVICE TIME time units.
If it is not possible to reach all customers, the better solution would be the one where most customers are attended.
You can use modern approaches to solve VRPTW problem, such as: Branch and Price, Branch and Cut, Dynamic Programming with State Space Relaxation, Lagrangian Relaxation, Heuristic Search with Relaxation.
For each vehicle, compute the visits one after another with these quantities, as floats:
- TRAVEL STARTS AT: the time the vehicle leaves the previous point (the depot or the previous customer).
- TRAVEL DISTANCE: the distance from the previous point to the customer.
- ARRIVAL TIME = TRAVEL STARTS AT + TRAVEL DISTANCE.
- WAITING TIME: if ARRIVAL TIME < READY TIME then READY TIME - ARRIVAL TIME, otherwise 0. The vehicle waits until READY TIME.
- LATE ARRIVAL TIME: if ARRIVAL TIME > DUE TIME then ARRIVAL TIME - DUE TIME, otherwise 0.
- ENDS DELIVERY AT = max(ARRIVAL TIME, READY TIME) + SERVICE TIME.
- ACCUMULATED DISTANCE: the distance travelled by the vehicle so far.
Remember that, for next travel, TRAVEL STARTS AT = ENDS DELIVERY AT. And all vehicles must return to the depot once they have finished the routes. This TRAVEL DISTANCE must be taken into account in the ACCUMULATED DISTANCE.
Give the Score of the solution. Final score = 10000 x unattended customers + sum of the ACCUMULATED DISTANCE of each vehicle + 1000 x sum of LATE ARRIVAL TIME + 1000 x units of load above the vehicle capacity. As you see, for calculating the score, the most heavily penalized items are the ones with the largest weights above. These items have no cost in the score: the number of vehicles used, WAITING TIME. Distances are Euclidean distances truncated to one decimal: each leg between two points measures floor(10 x d) / 10 distance units, where d is the exact Euclidean distance. Use these truncated leg distances in every calculation.
Time windows and vehicle capacity are hard constraints: the delivery must start no later than DUE TIME, and the load of each vehicle cannot exceed its capacity. A solution with any late arrival or any excess load is infeasible, and the penalty weights above only discourage that, they do not make it acceptable. Among feasible solutions, the best one is the one with the smallest total distance (truncated to one decimal).
A vehicle can visit one or more than one customer, not all vehicles must visit the same number of customers, and vehicles that visit no customer are not used. Have in mind that it can be better not using all vehicles, because this could lead to a better solution than using all vehicles.
